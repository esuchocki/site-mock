KCL Pricing — old vs new, one step at a time

A guide to what changed, why, and how it compares to the rest of the retreat world. Everything is explained first, then shown. Start with one guest, one night, change one thing at a time, and watch the dollars move.

First, what do "old" and "new" actually mean?

OLD

The pricing KCL used through 2025. A guest paid one all-in price per night — and that single number silently bundled the room and the meals together. Nothing on the bill ever said how much was room and how much was food; the database never recorded the split. A "Standard" room was simply $120/night, meals included but invisible.

NEW

The redesigned 2026 model. It splits that one number into two visible lines: a room rate (the bed) and a food rate (~$40/adult/night for three meals). Same guest, now an itemized bill: $160 room + $40 food = $200. The food was always being paid for — the new model just makes it countable.

So the central question this page answers: when we pull the hidden food out of the old price and set it beside the new itemized price, how much did the room itself really go up — versus how much of the "increase" is just food becoming visible? The three boxes below (A, B, C) separate those two things on purpose.

Three numbers, defined once: Room — the lodging charge alone (the bed). Food — the meals charge (3 meals/day). All-in — room + food, the total a guest pays per night.

Now the outside world: how KCL's new prices compare

The hard part of pricing is knowing whether your numbers are high or low for the market. Below, each KCL tier's new all-in (room + food) is set beside the closest real, published rate at comparable retreat centers. One caution, stated plainly: nearly every peer bundles meals into one number, so their figures already include food — we compare them to KCL's all-in (room + food), not the room alone, to keep it apples-to-apples.

How to read "where KCL sits" — each tier is judged against the full verified peer spread, not one number: under-priced = below the cheapest comparable peer; low in range = inside the spread but in its lower half; mid–high in range = upper half of the spread; above peer range = above the most expensive peer. Peer rates are 2025–26 published figures (meals included); full sourcing in competitive_pricing_report.html.

The honest, tier-by-tier read: against the full peer spread, KCL's premium and standard tiers land low in the range — below or near the bottom of where comparable private rooms sit (Drala $254, Garrison ~$262, Kripalu up to $599 for premium), which is the clearest case for raising the top tiers. The dorm and shrine tiers are competitive-to-firm — already at or above the cheapest peers once food is added, so leave them. One caveat to keep visible: the budget end depends heavily on the comparator (Drala's cheapest tiers are genuinely bare-bones), so read the low end as "no room to cut," not "raise aggressively." The structural oddity stands across all peers: KCL is moving to itemize food while nearly everyone bundles it — strongly consider a single all-in headline number with the breakdown underneath.

What this means for a whole year

One night is the building block; this scales it to the whole center. We multiply each tier's per-night change by the real number of stays and the real average length of stay for that tier, then add it all up. No guessing on volume — these are actual 2022–24 booking counts.

Why two yearly numbers? The guest-facing number counts the food that's now itemized; the room-only number strips food off both the old and new side so you see the real room increase, not the accounting change. Food revenue itself didn't appear from nowhere — it was always collected, just hidden inside the old room price.

Where every number here comes from

  • KCL room prices — room_inventory.csv (new 2026) and room_inventory_old.csv (old 2025), the real rate tables.
  • Booking mix — the live legacy database, 2022–24 average (≈900 paid stays/yr; staff, online, and commuter bookings excluded).
  • The two reconstructed numbers — only the sliders above are estimates: how much of the old price was secretly food, and the per-night adders. Everything else is real.
  • Competitor benchmarks — 2025–26 published rates from a two-pass, fact-checked market study; full tables and sources in competitive_pricing_report.html. Peer rates include meals, so they're compared to KCL's all-in (room + food).
