How to read this

  • Floor ($1,000 flat) is the most teacher-favoring model for small weekend workshops (median attended < 12), where any percentage-based model would yield less than $1,000. It is the cheapest for KCL on every other archetype — long retreats, mid-length programs, and high-draw events all generate enough net tuition that a flat $1k is the smallest payout among the six models.
  • P30 (30% of net tuition) and P50 (50%) scale with tuition revenue and have no floor. They reward high-enrollment programs and pay almost nothing for small or low-fill ones. P50 is the most teacher-favoring model in 22 of 30 programs (any program with non-trivial net tuition). P30 is the cheapest for KCL on 11 of 30 programs — exactly the high-revenue, short-duration ones where the absence of a per-night base helps KCL most.
  • Base+25 ($100/night + 25%) and P30 cross over at the threshold net tuition > $2,000 × nights. Below that threshold (24 of 30 programs in this sample), Base+25 pays the teacher more; above it (6 of 30, including Family Camp, Medicine Buddha with Anyen Rinpoche, and the 7-Day Meditation Retreat), P30 pays more. In aggregate the six "above-threshold" programs are heavy enough to flip the total: P30 pays $65k total vs. Base+25 at $70k — P30 is cheaper by $4,800.
  • SrBase+25 ($150/night + 25%) is identical to Base+25 plus $50/night. It only matters for long retreats — the Month-Long Meditation Retreat (27 nights) pays the teacher $4,000 more under SrBase+25 than Base+25; weekend workshops gain only $100. This is the structural lever for "pay senior teachers proportionally to commitment."
  • Hybrid ($75/night + 30%) is almost identical to a weighted blend of Base+25 and P30. It pays slightly more than Base+25 across the board and is teacher-favoring on most archetypes without the volatility of P50.

Reading by stakeholder

  • For senior teachers running flagship programs (Medicine Buddha, Month-Long Retreat, Shambhala Training): P50 pays the most, SrBase+25 second. Floor pays meaningfully less than any percentage-based model. If the policy goal is to compensate senior teachers proportionally to draw, Floor is structurally wrong for this archetype.
  • For new or low-draw teachers running small workshops (Kyudo, Tai Chi, small weekend workshops): Floor pays $1,000 regardless. Every percentage-based model pays less than $1,000 for these programs — sometimes much less ($250–$700). If the policy goal is to make small programs viable for the teacher, Floor or a floor-with-percentage-bonus structure is the only structurally fair option.
  • For Karmê Chöling as institution: Floor minimizes total honoraria ($30k aggregate) but leaves senior teachers underpaid. P50 maximizes them ($109k) and may not be sustainable without operational cost data. Base+25 and Hybrid sit in the middle — Base+25 has the lowest std dev of the percentage-based models, meaning its per-program payouts are the most predictable to budget.
  • For Family Camp specifically: this single program drives a spread of $21k across the six models — P50 would pay $22k, Floor would pay $1,000. Whatever uniform policy is adopted, Family Camp is the program where the dollar consequence is largest and most worth discussing separately.

Enrollment sensitivity — Base+25 model

Tuition and room & board scale with enrollment; the $100/night base is fixed regardless of headcount. The fixed base creates slightly-better-than-linear upside scaling.

Registration timing

By T−30 (30 days before start), the typical program is at 75% of its eventual headcount; by T−14, 92%. T−14 is the tighter empirical signal for the "will this program hit ≥8 final attendees" cancellation decision.

Notes

  • Sample. 30 most-recent residential paid programs (PROG_CATEGORY_CODE='REG', PROG_PRICE>$100, ≥3 arrived; online and test programs excluded). Spans 2025-05-09 to 2026-05-10. Length 2–27 nights; arrivals 3–45.
  • Net tuition = gross PROG charges − all discount lines and discount-flagged payments (includes program-staff comps at 100% discount). R&B = net ACCO charges bucketed by room type (KCL_*/AB* → on-site; PLATFORM/TENT/CAB T → tent; COMMUTE → commuter).
  • Paying attendees (340 of 433) excludes 93 program-staff registrations, which consume room/kitchen but pay no tuition.
  • KCL net = (net tuition + R&B) − honorarium. Operating costs (food, staff labor, utilities, overhead) are out of scope. This is revenue available before operating costs, not profit.
  • Floor pass. All 30 of 30 programs pass the conservative floor (honorarium ≤ net tuition). The binding constraint is operational cost, which is outside available data.
  • Capacity classifier. Accommodation type uses seasonal room codes (CAB T S, TENTSING, OTENTINC, PLATFORM) so tent overflow is correctly counted. Tent inventory concentrates in summer: Family Camp 2025 (25 in tents) and Medicine Buddha with Anyen Rinpoche (7) account for the majority.
  • Archetype assignment. Programs are grouped by length, headcount, and on-site room count using thresholds applied uniformly: small workshop (≤3 nights, <12 arrived); standard weekend (≤3 nights, ≥12); high-draw short (≥30 arrived in ≤4 nights, or ≥20 on-site rooms); mid-length retreat (5–9 nights, ≥12 arrived); mid-length low fill (5–9 nights, <12 arrived); long retreat (≥10 nights); Family Camp (matched by name).
