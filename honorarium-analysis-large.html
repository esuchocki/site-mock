Year-by-year trajectory and the statistical breaks

Programs and attendance per year across the full dataset window. Two structural breaks dominate: the Sunshine Report (mid-2018) coincides with a 26% drop in programs and 46% drop in attendance the following year. COVID (early 2020) coincides with a further 87% / 91% drop. The shaded background bands mark the three statistically-distinct operating eras used in the cohort analysis below.

The current era runs at roughly 14 attended per program vs. 37 pre-Sunshine — about a 61% drop in program scale. The forward policy decision should be calibrated against current-era economics; pre-Sunshine numbers are historical reference but no longer descriptive. Pre-Sunshine dollars are also nominal (not inflation-adjusted), so direct $-comparisons across eras are conservative.

Each line shows what one era would have paid in aggregate under each model. The pre-Sunshine line sits dramatically higher because that era ran roughly 8.5× the program volume of the current era. The shape of the curve — which models are cheap vs. expensive — is consistent across all three eras.

Program-size distribution by era

How program sizes have changed. The pre-Sunshine era was dominated by larger programs (15+ arrived); the current era is dominated by small and medium workshops. This shift compounds the per-program revenue drop and shifts which honorarium models matter most.

Notes

  • Sample. 488 qualifying programs starting between 2012-01-01 and 2026-05-20 (PROG_CATEGORY_CODE='REG', PROG_PRICE>$100, ≥3 arrived; online and test programs excluded). Pre-2012 data has no qualifying programs.
  • Statistical breaks. Year-over-year changes in arrived count: 2018→2019 = −46% (Sunshine Report); 2019→2020 = −91% (COVID). 2020–2024 swung between 5 and 36 programs/year with no stable level; 2025+ shows recovery to a new baseline. Cohort boundaries are drawn at these breaks: pre-Sunshine (2012–2018, stable), transition (2019–2024, chaotic), current (2025–2026, post-recovery).
  • Inflation caveat. Pre-Sunshine dollars are nominal. Approximately 25–30% cumulative US inflation between the pre-Sunshine midpoint (2015) and 2026 means $-comparisons across eras understate the pre-Sunshine real magnitude. Percentage-based models self-adjust to tuition price changes; fixed-dollar models (Floor, $/night bases) do not.
  • Eight models analyzed. Floor = $1,000 flat per program; Base+15 = $100×nights + 15% net tuition; Base+20 = $100×nights + 20%; P30 = 30% net tuition (no base); Base+25 = $100×nights + 25%; Hybrid = $75×nights + 30%; SrBase+25 = $150×nights + 25%; P50 = 50% net tuition (no base).
  • Net tuition = gross PROG charges − all discount lines and discount-flagged payments (includes program-staff comps at 100% discount). R&B = net ACCO charges. KCL net = (net tuition + R&B) − honorarium. Operating costs are out of scope.
  • Archetype thresholds applied uniformly across eras: small workshop (≤3 nights, <12 arrived); standard weekend (≤3 nights, ≥12); high-draw short (≥30 arrived in ≤4 nights, or ≥20 on-site rooms); mid-length retreat (5–9 nights, ≥12 arrived); mid-length low fill (5–9 nights, <12 arrived); long retreat (≥10 nights); Family Camp (matched by name).
  • Room utilization > 100% pre-Sunshine reflects tent overflow plus multi-occupancy in some KCL rooms. The 20-room cap is a baseline reference, not a physical hard limit.
  • Encoding fix. Legacy program/teacher names have cp437-over-utf8 mojibake; reversed before output (e.g., "Shambhala Training Levels 1 ΓÇô 5" → "Shambhala Training Levels 1 – 5").
