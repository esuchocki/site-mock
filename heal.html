A place to stop.

Rest has to do with stillness, which is the courage to not move away from one’s pain. Healing has to do with trusting you are good enough to not turn away from what you discover. Both of these are the meditation, and the ground of calm and insight.

A Wellness Sanctuary in Northeast Vermont

A contemplative space. A sanctuary. Over seven hundred acres of forest, field, and stream — held in the Shambhala Buddhist tradition for over fifty years. A restorative environment, removed from the speed and distractions of the modern world. A place where the body can soften and the mind can rest.

This Is How

Heart of Recovery

A weekend program for those in recovery from addiction — alcohol, drugs, food, relationships. Meditation, Buddhist teachings, and a compassionate community for the journey of sobriety.

Heart Space Yoga

Yoga, breathwork, somatic awareness, and sound healing. A nervous system reset for those carrying stored tension and trauma in the body.

Qigong & Self-Care

The Taoist three pillars of self-care: qigong, meditation, and lifestyle. Two-thousand-year-old practices for vitality, taught in the Xiantianwujimen lineage of Dr. Eva Wong.

Mudra Space Awareness

A body-based contemplative practice introduced by Chögyam Trungpa Rinpoche. Movement, gesture, and the recovery of full sensory presence in the body.

The Fullness of Being

A silent retreat in the tradition of Jon Kabat-Zinn’s Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR), held in the contemplative roots of mindfulness meditation. For burnout, illness, and meeting suffering.

Ikebana & Kado

Japanese flower arranging as contemplative art. One of the traditional contemplative disciplines introduced by Chögyam Trungpa Rinpoche to the Shambhala community at Karmê Chöling.
